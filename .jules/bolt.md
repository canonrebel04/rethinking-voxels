## 2024-05-18 - Safe Replacements for Expensive GLSL pow() calls
**Learning:** In GLSL, standard `pow(x, 2)` or `pow(x, y)` is often evaluated as `exp2(y * log2(x))`, which is slower than basic integer multiplication. While `pow2(x)` is sometimes available as a function, passing complex inline expressions like `max(0.0, 1.0 - x)` into it (or any macro) can carry the risk of double-evaluation or trigger micro-optimization concerns where modern compilers might already simplify `pow(x, 2.0)`. A universally safe, explicitly optimized pattern that avoids macro/compiler risks is extracting the complex expression into a local variable `float t = ...;` and then multiplying it explicitly `(t * t)`.
**Action:** Replace `pow(x, 2)` with local variable extraction and explicit multiplication (e.g., `float temp = x; result = temp * temp;`). This guarantees safety from macro double-evaluation and ensures explicit, measurable optimization. Also, always cast scalar literals to float (`0.0`, `1.0`) when working in a floating-point operation context to guarantee stability and syntax correctness across different GLSL compilers.
